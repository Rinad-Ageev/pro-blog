# A catalog on screen and a request in the system: what lies between them?

A retrospective of events on 18 and 20 September 2026. These are not development starting dates. Planned publication date: 19 October 2026 (Asia/Shanghai).

## The problem

The catalog is visible and a draft has been composed. But are the data current, will the request be retained, and can it be used in actual work? Those questions remain even when the screen looks convincing.

## Separating the checks

I propose three separate questions: what is visible, what persists, and what establishes readiness for work. This is a framework for discussion, not a description of an implemented architecture.

## Two different prototypes

An audit on 18 September documented catalog elements, filters, and local scenarios in a marketplace prototype.

A separate client prototype on 20 September prepared local cargo and processing request drafts in the current session. Capacity, rates, balance, and tracking were not populated with unverified figures.

I am comparing two results. Their date order does not establish that one product grew from the other, that they shared an architecture, or that data were transferred between them.

## What is confirmed

The basis is saved, dated reports: catalog elements and local scenarios in one case, session-local drafts in the other. This is bounded documentary evidence, not a fresh check of running systems.

## What remains open

A catalog snapshot does not establish live inventory, nor does a displayed price establish an offer. Full trading and an operational release are not established; the audit did not complete full browser, security, and production acceptance testing.

The session-local summary does not store the complete request. An interface route does not establish separation of different clients’ data. There were no external requests or production deployment of the client prototype at that stage. These materials do not establish long-term storage or actual operational use.

## The next step

For a future check, I would separately record the basis for data freshness, expected request retention, and access conditions for different clients, then compare those expectations with observations. This is a proposal, not an executed test.

What would need to be confirmed before you trusted a visible draft with a real request?
