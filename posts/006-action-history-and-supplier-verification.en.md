# An action history does not mean the supplier has been verified

![A blank sheet and card in a blue folder with an orange tab; a magnifier and empty tray stand apart, with a gap between the groups.](../assets/images/cover-006-v1.png)

*Conceptual AI illustration: a record’s origin and supplier verification are separate questions. This is not a screenshot of a running system or evidence of verification.*

A retrospective of the event on 29 September 2026 and a saved snapshot from 4 October. These are dates of historical evidence, not the start of development. Planned publication date: 27 October 2026 (Asia/Shanghai).

## A conclusion that can come too early

Supplier information has appeared in the portal. Can the supplier now be considered verified? I would first establish where the record came from, then separately ask whether verification has supporting evidence. The presence of information alone does not answer the second question.

## What needs to stay separate

An import records the arrival of information. Supplier verification needs its own basis. An assistant action also needs to remain distinct from a staff action: the history of one action type should not attribute it to a different participant.

This is a way to read an action history, not an account of a particular employee or a verified supplier.

## What the historical release added

On 29 September 2026, the procurement portal gained an action history and embedded guidance. Importing information was separated from supplier verification, and assistant actions from staff actions.

That result concerns recording the origin of actions. It does not confirm the quality or integrity of a real supplier.

## What the saved evidence supports

Saved machine evidence records the exact release and shows that history was closed to an anonymous request within the checked scope. Here I use a sanitized account of that historical evidence. No fresh check of the running portal was conducted for this entry.

One checked access boundary does not amount to a full security audit, a review of all current roles, or confirmation of privacy across the system.

## What remained outside the result

A known FAIL remained in the broader local test set, outside the published change. Successful checks of the exact release did not cancel it. I would not add different test sets together as a single readiness measure.

This release did not activate ERP or AI connections. In the saved 4 October snapshot, a newer local candidate had not been published and did not change the historical release. This account does not confirm current operation, savings, or business acceptance.

## A criterion I would propose next

For a future authorized check, I would separately record the information's origin, the action type, and the basis for a supplier-verification conclusion. I would then compare those expectations with observations. This is a proposal, not a completed test.
